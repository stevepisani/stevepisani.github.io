# The planet: how it's built

The homepage world, module by module, and how each thing a visitor can do works. Read the part
for whatever you're changing before you change it; the design rules and art direction it all
follows are in `CLAUDE.md` (keep them). How to see your change working: `.claude/skills/verify-world`.

three.js ES modules in `assets/js/world/`, bundled into one file by esbuild (`tools/build-js.mjs`);
`main.js` is the entry. Rapier is the physics engine for the loose things (`physics.js`).

## Modules

- `main.js`: panels, menu, top bar, history (works before and without 3D); then the renderer, picking (hover label + ground ring, click-to-walk, click-to-use; the hover and destination rings are draped on the ground vertex by vertex, over the flagstones and onto the bar's deck (`bar.solids`), and set where you'll really stand: `player.clearOf()` pushes the spot out of every collider by the ring's radius, and you walk there), sitting and leaving, hints, bloom + grade post-processing (desktop only). `#world[data-state]` = loading | walk | seat | camp | hammock | shore | note | fallback. Places you step into (the chalkboard, the hammock and a shelf of its sky, the telescope and a landing site) are history levels (`enterLevel` / `leaveLevel` / `backTo`): each adds a `#board`, `#hammock`, `#sky`, `#scope` or `#site` entry, so the browser's Back steps out of them one at a time; leaving any other way takes its entry (and any inside it, and a panel open over it) back off in one step. Asking the bartender (`chat` in `main.js`): a few questions are offered over the box until you've asked something (`ask` in `_data/bar.yml`, chips that ask when tapped); the box is 16 px, or iOS zooms into it; going to type turns you to face the bartender (`faceRobot()`: the same eased lean as watching a drink made, his face in the top of the view, the talk below it; you sit back a moment after you're done and he is), Ask and the questions don't take focus from the box (so the keyboard stays up and nothing moves mid-tap); while a phone's keyboard is up (the box has focus) the world fits what's left above it (`.is-typing`, fixed to `visualViewport`'s top and height; the canvas follows through a `ResizeObserver`), so nothing slides off the top. While he answers, his gauge flickers with the words, his lamps chatter and his head nods (`robot.talk()`); his answer's bubble, up under the top bar, points down at him.
- `planet.js`: radius-20 planet. `heightAt(dir)` is analytic, so terrain and the player's feet always agree. The visible terrain is a fine cube-sphere (about 0.3 m between vertices on desktop, 0.5 m on phones) with analytic normals and triplanar grain and bump from `textures.js` `groundDetail()`, worn to soil beside the trails; picking uses a coarse invisible copy named `ground` (clicks only need a direction). `place(obj, dir, {heading})` stands anything upright on the sphere; `dirFrom(polar, longitude)` addresses spots (the bar is at the north pole, facing longitude π/2). Also the atmosphere shell, instanced wind-blown grass tufts and pebbles, kept off trails and landmarks (`keepClear` from `places.js`). The lagoon (`POND`): a bowl carved into `heightAt` itself (an irregular outline, `pondReach`; waterline at `SHORE_K`, `shoreAt(phi)`), damp sand at the waterline and silt below it in the terrain colours, and `buildPond()`: a water disc on the sphere at `POND.level` whose edge the bowl's rim hides, dark and glassy (shader ripples bend its normal, the nebula tints it at grazing angles, lamp glints via `lampLit(water, { specular })`), reeds and cattails on the far shore and a few lily pads. `CUT` is the cutaway on the campfire trail's long straight (10 × 8 m, through the shell to the hollow inside at radius `inner`, 16.5 m; its frame: `up`, `e1` along the trail toward the fire, `e2`; `ends`, where the trail stops at each end of the bridge): `inCut(dir)` / `cutLocal(dir)`; the terrain, the scatter (`places.js`) and the physics ground (`physics.js`) all leave it open.
- `player.js`: first-person controller on a sphere. Orientation is a quaternion that is parallel-transported to the new up vector every step; never `lookAt` with a fixed world up (it flips at the poles). Collisions are circles in the tangent plane, tested only against colliders that are actually nearby in 3D (flattened onto the tangent plane, things on the far side of the planet land under your feet). `walkTo(point, { via })` auto-walks and turns you toward your path, through any corners in `via` without slowing; timing uses simulation time, not wall clock. Input: one pointer vocabulary for mouse and touch (press without moving = tap, press and move = look), plus WASD/arrows and E/Enter. A walk's distance to go is measured along the ground (the angle times the radius), not flat: the flat distance shrinks past a quarter of the way round, and a long walk would look stuck and give up.
- `bar.js`: Steve's, a classic A-frame tiki bar on a lava-rock plinth at the pole: carved tiki posts, steep thatched roof with an upswept ridge and Googie starburst, a neon "Steve's" script in the gable over a lit "World famous" box (dark letters on a glowing face, brass rim, marquee bulbs; `textures.js` `signBox()`), bamboo bar front, tapa-cloth back bar with a chalkboard of favorite drinks hung where you see it from your stool (`board`: its face, where each name is in its uv (`textures.js` `chalkboard()` records them), `rowAt(uv)`, and `mark(i)`, a chalk underline under a name; see the chalkboard below), floats and pufferfish lamps, volcano bowl, torches, the robot bartender, palms. `groundY(x, z)` gives the real ground height off the deck (the planet curves away). Seat pose and drink serving live here too: `serve(id)` has the robot shake at once, then at 0.9s slides a mug across the counter to your seat (placed directly under reduced motion); `greet()`/`farewell()` are called on sitting and leaving. `make(recipe, { onStep, onDone })` has the robot make a real drink from `drinks.yml` in front of you, one step at a time: it rolls up to the counter, sets out the glass with its ice (`props.js` `barGlass()`), pours each ingredient from its own tinted bottle (into a tin in the middle of the counter if the drink is shaken, straight into the glass if not; the level rises by each measure), then shakes and strains, stirs or swizzles (the glass frosts), garnishes (`garnishFor()`), and slides it over. Each thing stands under the hand that works it, and a pour stream only shows while the spout is really over the vessel. The bottles it pours Planteray, Campari, Chartreuse and Meletti from are the real ones: `bottle-*` hero models (`"lazy": true` in the manifest, so they load once the browser is idle after the reveal: `loadHeroes(…, { lazy: true })`, shaders compiled off to the side one bottle at a time, then `bar.setBottles()`), standing in a row at the robot's end of the counter until it picks one up (`REAL` maps a build line to its bottle); everything else pours from a tinted stand-in bottle. Every step has an enter, a per-frame part and a finish, so `skip()` (and a long frame) lands on the finished drink; `cancel()` clears it. `update(t, dt)`: `t` is ambient time (frozen by `?test`), `dt` real frame time for things the guest causes, so they still play under `?test`.
- `places.js`: the trail network (`TRAILS`: a flagstone walk from where you land to the bar, gravel trails from its fork to the rocket, telescope, boat and dish, and on round the back to the campfire, with lanterns along each, walk included, starting a few steps in so none looms as you land; none is a real light: each throws its pool through `lamps.js`), your ship, a Saturn V-style lander (`rocket.js`), on a lava-rock landing pad with chasing edge lights and floodlights up the hull, and beside it a launch console (`props.js` `launchConsole()`: a cream desk with a green-phosphor screen counting down to the next real launch from `window.nextLaunch()`, redrawn once a second; tap it for the launch panel), a brass refractor telescope on a wooden tripod, the campfire (`camp.js`), the radio dish ("Get in touch": Contact), the canoe (pulled up out of the lagoon, bow in the shallows; the lagoon is a collider, so you stop at the waterline), round the lagoon (`LAGOON` azimuths; a spur trail comes down beside the canoe) a striped hammock between two palms (leaning away from each other) with a book left open in it, and two more palms at the water's edge in front of it, leaning out over the lagoon and apart, which frame the view when you lie back (you climb in, lie back and read: the Reading list; see below) and messages in bottles washed up at the waterline (crouch behind them and write one, then throw it into space: see below), a little pile of flat stones where the lagoon trail comes down to the water (skip one: see below), a door standing on its own out past the telescope at the end of its own spur trail (`props.js` `doorway()`: carved tiki posts, a lintel with upswept ends under a little thatch, the door swung open, a brass label on a stake crediting the scan, a lantern; and in the opening, a real place in daylight: see below), a tiki signpost in the corner of the fork whose arrow boards point along the real trails (wayfinding, not clickable), and seeded scatter (kept off the trails: lava rock, palms, and `decor.js` `shrub()`s, one merged mesh per shrub) (`SPOTS` holds their positions). Interactables are `{ id, label, verb, object, extra, point, approach, radius }`: click `object` (or anything in `extra`), walk to `approach`, then use it (one that opens a panel first turns you to face it, `player.face()`, so it's in front of you when the panel closes); an `id` that's a panel id (`contact`, `shelf`) opens that panel (the launch console by the pad opens `launch`), so a landmark is a shortcut to a section that's also in the top bar and menu. The bartender mentions them (`_data/bar.yml`).
- `machine.js`: the cutaway (`CUT`): the planet is hollow, and a machine. The campfire trail stops at one end of the opening and goes on from the other (`TRAILS` `campfire` and `campfire2`); you cross on a steel truss bridge (a grating on a bottom chord, posts and diagonals, a handrail, four lanterns that are lamp pools), the grating running end to end so you see down through it as you cross. The cut's walls are the shell in cross-section (`strataTexture()`: moss, soil, lava rock, a brass seam, riveted hull plates down to a lit inner skin), a brass lip over the edge, a railing round the rest. Below, centred on the planet's centre (`inside`, drawn only within 40 m): the inner shell seen from inside (`shellMaterial()`: dark panels in rows, lit seams, windows of light like a city at night, conduits with light running round them), a churning white-hot core (`coreMaterial()`) with a halo, three gimbal rings turning on their own axes with beads of light, and ten spokes out to the shell carrying pulses of light (none under the opening). Everything inside is lit by the core in its own shaders (`coreLit()`), not by the moon, which has no shadow down there. Returns its `group` (the cut's frame: x along the bridge, y up), `inside`, `deck` (the bridge's deck, undrawn, in `main.js`'s pick list: a tap on the bridge is a tap on the ground, so you walk where you tapped; nothing on the bridge is a thing to use), `colliders` (both sides of the bridge and the edge railing, every 0.45 m), `solids` (the deck and the walls, for Rapier; a coconut thrown in falls through and turns up back under its palm, physics.js), `lamps`, `platform` (the middle of the bridge) and `core`. Stop halfway and look down.
- `rocket.js`: your ship, `saturnLander()`: a Saturn V cut down to about 3.7 m and built like the Outer Wilds ship. It keeps what makes it recognisable (the stack of stages narrowing to the command module and a red escape tower, the black-and-white roll pattern, "USA" and a flag, five engine bells, four fins) on a squat lathe body whose UVs run by height, so one canvas paints the livery in metres, plus hand-built wear (panel seams, rivet rows, patch plates, scuffs) and the SJPJr badge from the favicon, drawn in once it loads. Outer Wilds touches: four sprung landing legs (hinge, piston strut, round foot), a glowing porthole cockpit in a riveted brass ring, a hatch with a lit window and a ladder to the ground, thruster quads, a dish antenna. The front is local -z; `faceFront(x, z)` turns it to the path.
- `camp.js`: the campfire, Outer Wilds style. `buildCampfire()`: lava-rock ring, glowing coals, a log teepee (turned so no log stands end-on in front of you), two flames, sparks, log benches, and company: Rodin's Thinker (a nod to Philadelphia's Rodin Museum; a `thinker` hero model, `props.js` `thinkerFallback()` as the stand-in) seated on his rock in one place round the fire and the moai behind the log in another, either side of yours (`places.js` sinks both into the ground, which curves away from the camp's plane), a bag of marshmallows; `seatToward(x, z)` puts your bench toward the trail, lays both benches, the bag and the spare skewers on the real ground (`groundY` from `places.js`: the ground falls away from the camp's flat plane, so nothing hangs in the air) and returns the seat poses. `createRoaster(camera)`: the stick you hold (on the camera, so it follows your look); press and hold (Space on keyboards) reaches it into the flame, it toasts by distance to the hot spot (cream, golden, brown, black), catches fire past dark brown, and `eat()` gives a `verdict()`; each marshmallow comes with a line from `likes` in `_data/bar.yml`. In `main.js`, `sitAtFire()`/`leaveFire()` use the same continuous `flyPath` body motion as the bar; "Eat it" (or "Blow it out") bottom-right, "Leave the fire" bottom-left, Esc leaves; the roast hint retires once something's toasted.
- `paths.js`: trails built vertex by vertex on the analytic surface, so nothing floats or sinks: `sampleTrail(points)` (a smooth, gently meandering curve), a crushed-shell gravel ribbon with ragged edges (`trailTexture()`), flagstones draped on the terrain (top a few cm proud, sides running into the ground; `layout()` lays every slab by its real outline and never lets two touch: a slab that won't fit is tried smaller, then left out, so there's always a gravel joint; edge pebbles likewise), pebble borders, lantern spots, and `trailEdgeFn()` (distance to the nearest trail edge, for grass, scatter and ground wear). Triangles must face outward: a ribbon or slab wound the other way is culled and vanishes.
- `decor.js`: the layered density real tiki bars have (from Smuggler's Cove, Three Dots and a Dash, Trader Vic's, Tiki-Ti): a backlit rum wall (instanced lathe bottles with labels and caps) on every shelf, crossed paddles and a carved mask on the tapa, a sagging net of glass floats under the roof, a grass fringe and festoon bulbs on the front beam, rope trim on the bar top, the robot's station (citrus, tins, jigger, bitters, swizzles and umbrellas, towel), and monstera, red ti and fern beds around the plinth (entrance left open). Repeats are instanced and it adds no lights, so phones pay little. Leaves, net and rope textures live in `textures.js`.
- `robot.js`: the bartender, a 1950s tin-toy robot built from primitives, printed like the real ones: `textures.js` `tinLitho()` lithographs every panel (teal enamel, a cream and coral border line, printed rivets and seams, vents; chevrons round the skirt). `PALETTE.tinTeal` is bluer than teal on purpose, so it still reads teal under the amber lamps. Box head with a glowing Grog-o-Meter dial under a red visor, glass dome, an antenna on a coil spring, aloha shirt with a paper umbrella in the pocket, a lei of plumeria flowers (`plumeria()`) draped the way a real one lies: flat on the shoulder plate outside the head (which overhangs the neck), then down the chest in a U; a bow tie on the collar plate, three blinking belly lamps, jointed arms with pincers, a shaker and a bar towel, and one wheel on a chrome bellows under a red tin mudguard. Poses (rest, greet, shake, pour) are eased per joint; to make a drink, `hold()` puts a bottle, bar spoon, swizzle stick or garnish in a pincer (hung tipped down from it, so reaching out over a glass tips a bottle's neck into it) and `reach()` solves the arm joints (coordinate descent on the real joints, for where the robot is headed) to put the held thing's tip at a point, with `stir` / `swizzle` wiggles; `beckon(guest)` turns and waves as you walk up; left alone at rest it wipes down the counter every nine seconds or so; `greet(spot, guest)` rolls over and turns to face you when you sit; with reduced motion it snaps between poses and nothing moves on its own. A `robot` hero model can replace the body only.
- `sky.js`: dim fbm nebula dome, twinkling stars, the sun (a cool distant star, `sunDir`), shooting stars, and two bodies in their own shaders. One sun lights everything: the planet (main.js's key light), Earth and the moon all take their light from `sunDir`, so what you see of each one's lit side follows from where it sits against the sun. Earth and the moon go round in one plane through the sun (`ecliptic`, through `sunDir` and `GIANT_DIR`, Earth's direction), so they lie along one line across the sky. Earth is low on the left as you land, about 99° round from the sun: NASA's Blue Marble by day and Black Marble city lights by night (`assets/images/earth/`, 2048×1024 WebP, public domain; loaded from `data-earth` on the script tag and preloaded in `world.html`, so it's in before the reveal), with drifting fbm clouds, a glint off the oceans, a soft terminator and a thin blue rim of air. It's turned to the real hour (`turnEarth()`, again every minute): the real subsolar point (`subsolar(date)`) faces this sky's sun, so it's day where the sun is really up and the cities are lit where it's really night. The sun is above it on screen, so north can't point up; it's tipped toward you instead, the northern hemisphere in view. `earthPoint(lat, lon)` is where a place is on the globe (in the Earth mesh's frame, matching three's sphere UVs): the hook for showing places (Google Maps, `docs/roadmap.md`) on it. The cratered moon (`textures.js` `moonMaps()`, Lommel-Seeliger shading, Earth's shine on its dark side) is in tonight's real phase by position, not by a light of its own: `site.js` `moonTonight()` gives the phase, and the moon is placed that far round its orbit from the sun (new beside it, full opposite it; the orbit is tilted 5° from the ecliptic so a new moon passes beside the sun). So it moves night to night: up ahead as you land for part of the month, over the far side of the planet for the rest. `bodies` gives the telescope where the moon, the sun and Earth are, how wide each looks and how far. Distant bodies set `fog: false`.
- `materials.js`: the palette (`PALETTE`) and every surface: procedural canvas textures (wood grain, bamboo, thatch, lava rock, tapa cloth, rattan, scuffed tin) on PBR materials, `enamel()` and `chrome()` for the robot, `glow()` for things that should bloom, `restyle()` for loaded Kenney models. Use these; don't introduce ad-hoc colours.
- `props.js`: procedural tiki props: carved tiki (a height-map face displaced into real geometry), glass floats in rope nets, fallen coconuts (`coconut()`), pufferfish lamps, tiki mugs, volcano bowl, bamboo tiki torches (tapered pole with nodes, rope lashing, a flared split-bamboo cup round a fuel canister and wick), palms with ringed trunks, alpha-textured fronds and a couple of dead ones hanging, lava rocks, moai; out on the planet, `radioDish()` (a retro radio telescope: pedestal, yoke, parabolic dish with seams, ribbed back, feed horn on struts, equipment hut) and `outriggerCanoe()` (hull with raised ends, float on two booms, paddle), `barGlass(kind)` (rocks, collins or coupe, with fill, ice, foam, frost and garnish) and `garnishFor(name)` (pineapple wedge, orange slice, mint sprig, coffee beans) for the drinks the robot makes, `hammock(a, b, up)` (striped canvas, `textures.js` `hammockCloth()`, fan lines, an open book with printed pages, `bookPage()`; `userData.hammock` lets you get in: the cloth, lines and book hang in a `swing` group that swings about the tie line as a damped pendulum (`kick()`, `update(dt)`), `setLoad(k)` sags it under your weight, `point(u, lift, across)` gives spots on it in the swing's frame), `bookStack()`, `messageBottles()`, `signpost(boards)` (lettering from `signLettering()`). `fire.js`: shader flames on crossed quads + flickering lights (`light: 0` = flame only).
- `lamps.js`: lamp light without lights. Lanterns and the walk's torches `addLamp()` a position and strength; `lampLitTree()` gives every standard material (`lampLit(m, { specular })` adds their glints, for water) on the planet and around the landmarks a shader loop that adds their warm pools (soft falloff, wrapped Lambert, a little flicker from `updateLamps(t)`, max 48, 5.5 m range). Same cost however many lamps, so phones get lit paths too; use it rather than adding real lights out on the planet.
- `sound.js`: sound, all synthesised with Web Audio (no audio files), each source placed on the planet with three.js `PositionalAudio` so it gets louder as you walk up: a quiet generated exotica loop at the bar (rolled vibraphone chords with motor tremolo, upright bass, bongos, the odd whistled bird call), the fire's rumble and crackles, water lapping at the lagoon, crickets everywhere; and effects from `bar.setSfx()` (the shaker, ice into a glass, pours while the stream is showing) the hammock's ropes creaking, and thuds and splashes wherever a loose thing lands (one movable source). The speaker button in the top bar (`#sound-btn`, next to the sections or the Menu button) starts it; it's off until asked for, remembered in localStorage `world-sound` (a remembered "on" starts on the first click or key, as browsers require), and suspended while the tab is hidden.
- `hoop.js`: the hoop. Pick up a coconut a second time and a neon basketball hoop flickers into being straight in front of you, about 5 m off. If that spot would sit on or against something, `hoopSpot()` in `main.js` turns out either side, a few degrees at a time, no further than the edge of your view (the camera's horizontal field, less a margin), for ground clear of the landmarks (`nearLandmark`) and off the walkway of any path (beside one is fine), out of the lagoon, 7.5 m from the bar and clear of every collider, loose thing and approach. If nothing in view is clear it waits (`hoopWanted`) and looks again a few times a second while you carry the coconut, so it appears the moment you face open ground, never behind you. It stutters on like a neon tube catching (`FLICKER_IN`: three short stutters, under three flashes a second), each with a flare of pink light round the rim (a sprite, no real light: a new light would recompile every shader) and a crackle (`sound.js` `zap`), then settles with a shower of sparks off the rim; under reduced motion it just fades in, no flashing. Its neon is the sign's: a pink rim, aqua round the backboard. While it stands, its pole, backboard and rim are solid (`physics.addFixed`, removed when it goes) and the pole is one of your colliders. A coconut that comes down through the rim (crossing its plane inside the ring, between frames) scores: four fireworks over it (`Points` with a soft round sprite, a crack and crackle each, `sound.js` `firework`), its neon flashing, then it stutters back out (`FLICKER_OUT`). Two more coconuts and it comes back. `window.__world.hoop` (`state`, `rim`, `up`) and `window.__hoopScored` for tests.
- `route.js`: walking round things, anywhere on the planet. A tap, a thing tapped or a coconut to pick up (`routeTo()` in `main.js`) gets a route when the straight line would run into the player's colliders (the counter, the staff-only area, posts, palms, torches, rocks, the machine's railings): `onPlanet()` lays a strip of ground along the great circle from you to the goal (8 m to either side, 4 m past each end; x along it, z across, in metres), marks the colliders in it on a 0.25 m grid, runs A* across it, then pulls the corners tight (`findRoute`). So from behind the bar a tap on it walks you round to the front and sits you down, a tap on the far end of the bridge walks you along it, and a tap on the campfire from where you land goes round whatever's between. A few milliseconds, up to about 20 for the far side of the planet; empty when the way is straight.
- `physics.js`: Rapier (WebAssembly) for the loose things: the fallen coconuts under the palms (`props.js` `coconut()`, `bar.loose` under the front palms and `places.loose` under the scattered ones) and two glass floats washed up by the bottles. Rapier has one gravity vector, so world gravity is zero and each body is pulled toward the centre every step (9.8 m/s², like the stones). Colliders: the ground as a trimesh of the analytic surface (a cube-sphere built with its index, about 0.65 m between vertices, lagoon bowl included), the player's collider circles stood up as posts (`height` where a thrown thing should clear them, e.g. the counter; `walkOnly` ones, like the lagoon's waterline and the staff-only area, are skipped), the bar's plinth (`bar.solids`), and you, a kinematic capsule at your feet, so walking into a coconut knocks it along. In the lagoon (`pondK < 1`, under `RADIUS + POND.level`): buoyancy by how deep it's in (`buoyancy`: coconuts 1.6, floats 4), water drag, and a slow current back to the sand. On dry ground, slow things drag to a stop in the grass and sleep (Rapier won't on a slope); they start asleep, lying where they were put, until disturbed. `on('thud' | 'splash', at, k)` for sounds (`sound.js`) and ripples (`stones.js` `ripple`). Long frames run up to four sub-steps. It loads once the browser is idle after the reveal (`load()`, `import()` of the engine), so its start-up never lands on your first steps, and nothing can be picked up until then. In `main.js` (the carry block): click one and you walk over and pick it up, held at arm's length (`CARRY`, on the camera) while you walk about; "Hold to throw" bottom-right (or Space): held longer is harder (full at 1 s), it goes where you look; "Put it down" bottom-left or Esc sets it at your feet, and anything that takes you out of the walk state puts it down first. The throw hint retires after the first throw. You and the skipping stones stay hand-written (`player.js`, `stones.js`).
- `booksky.js`: the book sky. Every book Steve has read (`/library.json`: `read`, from `tools/library.mjs`; no telling how he read it) is a star. Walking about, they're loose: scattered over a cap `LOOSE` (70°) round the hammock view, small, pale and rayless, nothing to tell them from the field's stars (that's the point: it should be a quiet surprise that they were there all along). Lie back in the hammock and they drift together (`update(…, gather)`: over `GATHER`, 7 s, each setting off a little after the last, easing in and out, along the sky's shell) into their shelves over the hammock, taking on their shelf's colour, size and rays as they come; only once they've settled (`settled`) do the names and lines fade in and can a shelf be leaned in on. Get up and they drift apart again; under reduced motion they're simply there. Settled, they're grouped by shelf, the shelves of /bookshelf and `_data/books.yml` in the same order. Each shelf is a disc just big enough for its books, and the discs sit in two rows in shelf order, left to right, like a bookcase (the split that keeps the rows most even), filling the window between the shore palms (`WIN`: 30° each side, 16° up and down, its middle 5° below the lying view's centre; the fronds hang across the top). With more books every star sits a little closer (`GAP` from 3.4° down to no less than 1.0°) rather than spreading behind the palms. Each shelf's name is in letter-spaced caps in its colour, above its patch on the top row and below it on the bottom one. A star's colour is its shelf's (`hue` in `books.yml`, pale enough to read as starlight), its size the book's length (8 to 13 px; 10 with no length); each is a small core that just blooms, with faint rays. A series of two or more inside a shelf is a constellation: its books in order, joined by faint lines, its name beside it once you've leaned in on that shelf (a name is left out if it would cover another's, biggest series first). What he's reading now (`now`) are comets crossing the whole field, three in flight at a time (`COMETS`), most recently played first: each takes a minute and a half (`CROSS`), fading in and out at the ends, then the next takes its turn (under reduced motion the first three sit mid-way, still). Laid out deterministically (hashed by book and series). Stars are at fixed world directions, on the sky's shell (the group rides on the camera like `sky.js`). The cone is centred on the lying view (`main.js`): you lie across the hammock looking up and out over the lagoon, between the two shore palms, whose trunks rise up the left and right edges; on a phone the middle of it is in view and you drag for the sides. If you move the hammock, those palms or the lying pose, re-check that framing (`verify-world`). `pick(x, y, …, reach, inRegion)` finds the star under a tap (screen space), `regionAt(x, y)` the shelf (its name, padded to 44 px, or its patch), `aim(r)` where to look and how narrow a view to lean in on it, `setFocus(r)` fades the other shelves back and brings up its series' names, `select(b)` lights one star and its constellation. Data: `/library.json`, fetched once the planet's up; no data, no stars.
- `batch.js`: fewer draw calls, which on a phone cost more than triangles. The bar and the landmarks are about 1,100 small meshes but under 200 looks; meshes that keep still and look alike (same material settings, even if made separately) are merged into one per patch of the planet about 10 m across, so what's out of view is still culled. Something that moves as a whole (a palm's crown swaying) is merged in its own frame and moves as one. What keeps still is measured, not guessed: `main.js` notes where everything is on the first frame and bakes on the fourth, before the reveal. The originals stay in place on layer 31, which the camera doesn't draw (the pick rays look at every layer), so picking, glints and code that holds on to them still work. Each frame `watch()` takes a batch apart the moment any of its originals moves in its frame, hides, swaps or alters its material (checked every half second) or has its geometry rewritten, and draws the originals again. `userData.noBatch` keeps a subtree out: the chalkboard, the menu card, and the robot's lamps, antenna, towel, shaker and tools. About 650 draw calls a frame became about 300 on a phone, 1,450 became 610 on a desktop (`window.__batched`, `window.__world.batcher` for tests).
- `glints.js`: now and then, something you can use catches the light: a quick four-pointed sparkle on it (one at a time, only things in view between 2 and 18 m, never the same one twice running), never announced. Out walking it picks from what you haven't used yet (`used` in `main.js`; the bar itself never glints, it's lit already); seated, the chalkboard until you've read it. The more different things you've used, the more often they come (every 9 s or so at first, down to about 3.5). Under reduced motion it fades in and out without turning or growing.
- `portal.js` and `realplaces.js`: the door onto a real place (see "Through the door" below). `portal.js` is its own bundle (`dist/portal.js`: Spark, the Gaussian-splat renderer, with a three.js of its own, 1 MB gzipped), loaded by URL only when someone steps through, never imported by the planet; `realplaces.js` holds the facts both sides need (the splat, the panorama, the credit, where you come in), so neither imports the other.
- `eyepiece.js`: the telescope's live views: NASA's moon this hour and today's sun, laid over the drawn ones, and the moon's landing sites (see the telescope below).
- `stones.js`: skipping stones. `stonePile()` for the shore; `createSkipper(scene)` flies them with no physics engine: a point under the planet's gravity (toward its centre), in 1/240 s steps; meeting the water (a sphere `RADIUS + POND.level` from the centre, inside the waterline `shoreAt`), it skips if it comes in under about 25° and over 2.2 m/s across, keeping 78% of its speed across and 55% of its speed down (as up), and leaves a ripple ring; otherwise it sinks with a plop, or lands on the sand (on the far side: "all the way across"). In `main.js` (`goToShore()` / `leaveShore()`, state `shore`): walk down to the pile and crouch at the water, a stone in your hand; press and hold (canvas, Space, or the "Hold to throw" button bottom-right) to wind up, let go to throw: held longer is harder (full at 1.1 s), where you look is where it goes, released low and level just over the water's edge the way stones skip. A short hold sinks, the sweet spot (about 0.7 s) gets 4 or 5 skips, full power makes the far shore. How it went is said in the bubble, your best is kept in localStorage `world-best-skips`, and the hint retires after the first skip. "Leave the shore" bottom-left or Esc stands you back up where you walked in.
- `note.js`: a message in a bottle, all in your hands (camera space, so the camera never moves for it). `createNoteRitual()`: `open()` picks up a bottle (`props.js` `messageBottle()`), pulls the cork, tips it and slides the letter out, sets the bottle down and unrolls the letter (a segmented plane wound round a tightening roll, `set(u)` from rolled to flat) where it fits the view; `rect()` is where it is on screen; `close(text, signed)` paints the words onto the paper (parchment canvas, ruled lines, Caveat), rolls it up, puts it back in and corks it; `throwIt()` sends it in an arc out over the lagoon and then, at the top of the arc, the planet lets go and it drifts off into space, tumbling. The letter glows a little (it's out of reach of the lanterns). Each stage is a timed step (reduced motion lands on the end of each). In `main.js` (`goToBottles()` / `leaveBottles()`, state `note`): crouch behind the bottles facing across the water and up a little; it opens at once, and the `#note` form sits exactly over the unrolled paper (`placeNote()`, every frame, `--gap` = the paper's line spacing) so you write on it: a transparent textarea in Caveat, "Signed (optional)", "N left", "Put it back" and "Roll it up" (Ctrl/Cmd+Enter). Rolling it up sends it to `public.bottles` (unapproved until Steve approves it; the 200-waiting cap's message is said if it's full); then "Throw it" bottom-right (Space/Enter/E) and the bubble says where it went; "Write another" starts again. Esc while writing puts the letter back; Esc otherwise or "Leave the shore" bottom-left stands you up. Tests (`?test`, automated browsers) never write to the table (`window.__lastBottle`).
- `fireflies.js`: everyone else on the planet right now, as fireflies: Supabase Realtime presence on the `planet` channel over a plain WebSocket (the Phoenix protocol, no client library). You join under a random key made fresh per page load and track only a rounded direction on the planet, when you've moved about a metre (at most every 4 s); others drift over to theirs a metre and a bit above the ground, bobbing and blinking slowly (still under reduced motion). At most 200, one `Points` object. It leaves while the tab is hidden and reconnects with backoff.
- `render.js`: pmndrs `postprocessing` + `n8ao`: AO → HDR-only mipmap bloom (threshold 1) → AgX tone mapping → vignette + grain → SMAA, then an untouched `overlay` scene on top (the menu card in your hands). Phones skip AO, grain, and shadows.
- `hero.js`: optional hero props (AI-generated or hand-made GLBs) in `assets/models/hero/`, listed in `manifest.json`; each slot falls back to its procedural version. Slots, prompts, manifest options (`height`, `rotateY`, `roughness`, `tint`, `brightness`, `glow`) and specs: `tools/models/HERO_PROMPTS.md`; sources and licences: `tools/models/README.md`.

## What a visitor does, and how it works

- Content stays HTML: the top bar and menu items are `<a href>`s (JS intercepts them to open `<template id="panel-<id>">`, and serves a drink if you're seated), so it works without WebGL and is crawlable. Without WebGL2 the menu is the page. Once the panels are in charge, `main.js` points those links at `#<id>` (their real hrefs: site pages, the résumé PDF, and outbound ones like mailto:) and `fallback()` restores them, so nothing in the menu or the drink animation ever leaves the page; the no-WebGL menu marks its outbound items ↗. Links inside panels that leave the page say so ("(PDF) ↗").
- The telescope (`lookThroughScope()` / `leaveScope()` in `main.js`, state `scope`): walk up and stand behind it, looking down at the eyepiece with the tube rising past it, then bend to it. As you step up, the telescope itself swings round to its first sight (`aimMount()`: its `mount`'s az and alt groups from `places.js`, at most about 70°/s, with its gears' `ratchet` from `sound.js`; left alone it goes back to slowly sweeping the sky); where its eyecup will be once it has (`eyecupAt()`, `userData.eyecup`) is where you stand and bend. As your eye comes to the eyecup the view goes dark (`#eyepiece-dark`, `--dark`, by how close it is, `eyeNear()`), and it opens again already through the eyepiece, on what it's pointed at (`#eyepiece`: a brass ring engraved with "STEVE'S" and the eyepiece's power, which follows the zoom; inside it, a soft fall-off toward the edge and a faint colour fringe at it). The camera's near plane moves out to 2 m while you're at the glass, so you look out of the telescope, not at its insides. Stepping back does it the other way: dark, then you straighten up from the eyepiece. What it can be pointed at comes from `sky.js` `bodies` (where each is, how wide it looks, how far): the moon when it's more than 10° above the horizon from here, the sun, and Earth (with a few cities pinned where they are, each with its time there now, a warm dot where it's day and a pale ring where it's night, Philadelphia ringed: `CITIES`, `earthPinsFrame()`, `#earth-pins`; the card says only "Right now."), each narrowed onto until it fills about half (the moon), three fifths (the sun) or nine tenths (Earth) of the field (`scopeFov()`). The moon and the sun are the real ones (`eyepiece.js`): the moon is NASA's picture of it this hour (Dial-A-Moon, through `supabase/functions/sky`, kept for the hour in localStorage `world-moon`), laid exactly over the drawn moon and turned so its lit side faces this planet's sun, with a ring at each Apollo landing site (`_data/moon.yml`), placed by the moon's real libration and tilt (`moonSpot()`; dashed where it's night there, hidden near the limb). Tap a ring and the view narrows three times further onto it, its name beside it, and the card tells you about it; ‹ › step through the sites, "The whole moon" or Esc goes back. A site that's in the dark tonight is shown on the nearest full moon instead (the function returns that hour's picture too, `full`), so a close look is never at nothing; the card says so ("It's night there now. This is it at full moon, 25 September."). The view stays centred on the site, whichever picture it's on. The sun is SDO's white-light picture of today's sun (sunspots), through a solar filter, so the sky round it goes black. Both pictures shimmer a little, like a real night's seeing (not under reduced motion). Nothing is fetched until you look through the telescope; without a connection, or if NASA doesn't answer, the eyepiece shows the moon and sun as the sky draws them. A card (`#scope-card`: below the field on phones, to its right on wide screens) says what you're looking at in a few words ("Waning crescent · 14% lit"), with a "Live · NASA" tag when the picture is the real one, and a line while it's being found ("Finding it live…") or if it can't be ("Live view unavailable. Showing tonight's phase."); × puts it away for the whole view, and a tap brings it back. The ring's engraving gives the power in an eyepiece set's steps (30×, 60×, 120×, 240×). ‹ › swings the telescope to the next one: the text backs up the view, it isn't the way in. The next launch is on the console by the rocket, not here. "Step back" bottom-left, Esc or Back widens the field back out, then stands you up where you walked in. A drag moves the telescope a little, within about a third of the field, like nudging one by hand; ← → and a swipe on the card do what ‹ › do. Each swing of the mount buzzes on phones that can (`buzz()`, Android), and settling on a site clicks. If nothing's above the horizon, the telescope just opens the panel. Nothing stands in its sight: the bar's third palm (`PALMS` in `bar.js`) leans in over the bar rather than out, because leaning out its crown crossed the moon's path from here. If you move the telescope, those palms or the sky's bodies, check every place the moon can be from the eyepiece (a ray per step round its orbit) again.
- The chalkboard (`lookAtBoard()`, `showRecipe()`, `leaveBoard()` in `main.js`): tapping it (seated, or from anywhere on the planet: you walk over and sit first) leans you in on it: your head turns to it and the view narrows until it fills it, the same eased lean as watching a drink made (`view`: its eye, aim and fov ease after their targets, so going from the board straight to the work is one head turn). It sits up top on a tall screen and to the left on a wide one, leaving room for the recipe. Its names are the tap targets (a raycast on the board's face, its uv to a row: `bar.board.rowAt`); one is underlined in chalk and its recipe comes up on a chalkboard card (`#board-card`: name, origin and glass, the build, method, note; below the board on phones, to its right on wide screens), with ‹ › through the board's drinks and "Make me one", which turns you straight to the robot to watch it made. "Back to the bar" bottom-left, Esc (the card first, then the board) and Back sit you back. Picking a drink taps the board with chalk (`sound.js` `chalk`, and a buzz where the phone can); ← → and a swipe on the card step through the drinks; the board's names are also unseen buttons (`#board-list`), for keyboards and screen readers. The bar's chatter and the question box wait while you read. The hint "Tap a drink to see how it's made." retires on the first pick. The menu's "Favorite drinks" still opens the panel, with its "Make me one" buttons.
- "Make me one" (`startMaking()` in `main.js`): the robot makes that recipe (`bar.make`), and a caption above the bottom buttons (`#making`: the drink, step n of N, what it's doing, the recipe's ingredients as chips, each lit while it's poured and ticked once it's in, progress dots; shown on phones too, where the bubble isn't) narrates it. "Skip to the drink" bottom-right or Esc jumps to the finished drink; "Leave the bar" stops it. From anywhere on the planet it walks you to your stool first. While it's made you lean in over the counter (the view narrows to about half its field, two thirds on phones so the whole workspace fits) and your eyes follow the work: the vessel being poured into, the shaker while it shakes, the glass for the rest, then the drink as it slides over (`focus()` on the job, eased like a head turn; snapped under reduced motion); you sit back once it's in front of you. Dragging to look is off while you lean in, and the menu (card, Menu button, E) finishes the drink first. Ordering from the menu meanwhile finishes the drink first; the bartender's chatter waits.
- The book sky (`zoomTo()` / `zoomOut()`, `openStar()` / `closeStar()` in `main.js`): only from the hammock, lying back, once the stars have gathered (`skyGather` turns on as you lie back, off when you get up). On the whole sky, hovering a shelf says its name and how many books; tapping it (its name, its patch, or near it: `regionAt(…, { near: true })` takes the nearest shelf within twice its size) leans in: the view turns to it and narrows to fit it (`camera.fov` eases from `BASE_FOV` to its `aim()` fov, 18° at the narrowest, over 1.1 s; snapped under reduced motion), the swing stills under you (the lying pose blends to the hammock at rest, or the stars would swing too), the other shelves fade, and its books' titles come up beside their stars (`#sky-labels`, DOM labels laid out again only when the view moves: as many as fit without touching each other or the buttons, the open book's first, then newest first). A drag pans, slowed by how narrow the view is. `#sky-shelf` above the bottom buttons names the shelf, steps ‹ › from shelf to shelf in order, and × leans back out. Once the view settles on the shelf, its newest book's card opens by itself (`zoom.openFirst`), so you see a real book without hunting for a star; stepping ‹ › to another shelf does the same. The card's ‹ › step books and say where you are on the shelf ("3 of 17"), the strip's step shelves; a swipe on the card and ← → step books too; opening a book turns a page (`sound.js` `page`); the shelf's books are also unseen buttons (`#sky-list`). Leaned in, tapping a book opens its card: a title on screen is its tap target (padded to 44 px tall, `labelAt()`), and so is the nearest star within 64 px (44 with a mouse). The card (`#star-card`) sits above the strip, which stays: cover (or its title on a square in the shelf's colour), title, author, series, "Science Fiction · Read March 2026" (just the shelf when undated) or "Reading now, 42% through", "Hear a sample" where there is one (Audible's public clip, `site.js` `playSample()`, one at a time), and ‹ › through the rest of that shelf, newest first, the order of /bookshelf (turning to a star that's off screen). A comet is a book from the whole sky too. Esc or × puts the card away first, the next Esc leans out; Back leans out too; getting up leans out. The Reading list's shelf headings (`data-shelf`) lean in on that shelf from the hammock, and go to /bookshelf#shelf anywhere else. Hints: "Every star above you is a book Steve has read. Tap a group to see its books." as you lie back (retires on the first lean in), then "Tap any title or star to see that book." (retires on the first card you open yourself).
- The hammock (`lieInHammock()` / `getOutOfHammock()` in `main.js`, state `hammock`): the same kind of continuous first-person body motion as the bar and the fire. Step up to its side, turn round and sit on its edge (it takes your weight: `setLoad`), swing your legs up and in and lie back across it, looking up and out over the water between the two shore palms, at the book sky; it swings from you getting in and you swing with it until it settles (the lying pose lives in the swing's frame; not under reduced motion). Lying back, you look up: the stars come first (show, don't tell), drifting together into their shelves (`lookUp()`; the hint says "Aligning…" until they've settled, then what to do with them). The text is there to support them, not to be the way in: "Reading list" bottom-right (or E) picks up the book left in the hammock, held open above you, and the Reading list (`panel-shelf`) pours out of it; closing it lowers the book to your chest and you're back with the stars. "Get up" bottom-left or Esc gets you out: the reverse, with the book back where it was, ending where you walked in, facing the hammock. Drag to look around while you lie there. The half turns (standing to sitting on the edge, and back) take 1.8 s, and as you swing your legs in you turn to face along it, then settle back across it, so the view sweeps round rather than tipping over the top of your head (which would flip it); getting in and out both peak at about 133°/s, inside the 140°/s limit.
- Asking the bartender (`#chat` / `#chat-log` in `index.html`, the chat block in `main.js`): while you're seated with nothing else open, a question box sits at the bottom (the Leave button's row on desktop, just above it on phones) and the conversation sits up under the top bar as a short transcript, the robot's answers pointing down at him, your questions on the right and his on the left, on phones too; the oldest fade out at the top and scroll. Up there it leaves the counter clear, so the menu card and its "Pick up the menu" label stay in view and in reach, and a tap beside a bubble goes through to the bar. An answer shows as it's said (the function streams it as plain text), with a pulsing ellipsis until the first words land; the transcript goes when the box does and comes back with it. The first question stops the rotating chatter. It posts the conversation (last 12 turns, `stream: true`) to the Supabase function `supabase/functions/bartender` (Claude Haiku 4.5, `max_tokens` 160, asked to stay under 40 words), which knows the site from `/bartender.json` (`bartender.json` at the root, built from `_data`: profile, career, menu, drinks, posts, lab, books, the bartender's lines), answers in a sentence or two, and keeps to the database's limits (`bartender_take()`: 1,000 replies a month, about $5, and 20 questions an hour per visitor, a daily-salted hash of the IP; `bartender_spent()` logs tokens in `bartender_usage`). If it can't answer, the robot's line says the bar's closed for a moment. Typing in the box doesn't move you (arrow keys, WASD), and Esc there only leaves the box. When the model can't answer (no credit, an outage, offline), the house answers from the site's own facts instead (`barback.js` `houseAnswer()`, from `/bartender.json`: a few kinds of question by keyword, the menu for the rest).
- Ordering at the bar (`order()` in `main.js`): choosing an item puts the menu back down so you can watch the robot shake the drink and slide it over (`bar.serve(id, onServed)`), then the mug lifts to you, tipped so you look into the drink, and the section's panel pours open out of the mug's mouth (`.panel.is-pouring`, a clip-path circle from `--ox/--oy`). Closing it sets the mug back down and the menu comes back up. Drinks come in the tiki mug (open top, so there's a drink to look into), coloured per section; the parrot and pineapple mugs dress the counter and shelf. Reduced motion skips the flights.
- At the bar the menu is a real card on the counter (`bar.menu`), and it is the HTML menu: `textures.js` `paintMenuCard()` paints its face from the laid-out `.menu__card` (every box, line of text, colour, focus state and its proportions), repainted on resize, pick-up and put-down. Sitting down leaves it there; clicking it (or E/Enter, or the Menu button) picks it up: `main.js` flies the 3D card to the exact rectangle the HTML menu will fill, facing the camera, then fades the HTML menu in over it and hides the card only once the page is opaque (no frame with neither). While it flies it's drawn in `render.js`'s `overlay` pass, after the grade, going from how the lamps tint it on the counter (`bar.menu.light()`) to the page's own colours, so the handoff doesn't change a pixel. × / Esc fly it back down. Clicking the card from anywhere walks you over, sits you down and picks it up. The camera never moves for this; reduced motion skips the flight.

## Models, libraries, tests, performance

- Models: Kenney CC0 props merged into `assets/models/props.glb` by `tools/build-models.mjs`, which only includes models the world code clones by a literal `prop('<kit>_<name>')` (just the coconut now; the rest stay in `tools/models/` as sources). ## Through the door

Out past the telescope, at the end of its own spur trail (`TRAILS` `door`, `SPOTS.door`), a door stands on its own in the grass, open onto a real place in daylight: the Sala Thai at the East-West Center in Honolulu, a Gaussian-splat scan by Andrew.HD (SuperSplat, CC BY 4.0; cropped, so the credit says so).

- **The opening** (`props.js` `doorway()`) draws the place from a panorama (`assets/splats/sala-view.webp`, 2048 × 1024, from where you come in). Each pixel looks the way you're looking through it, as if the place were painted on a sphere 9 m out from where you'll stand (`far`), so the view widens as you back off and shifts as you walk past, like a real doorway. The panorama loads a few seconds after the planet's up; until then the opening is the place's sky colour. Its daylight spills onto the step and the grass (an additive pool), and a lantern stands beside it like every landmark's.
- **Stepping through** (`stepThrough()` / `stepBack()` in `main.js`, state `door`): tap the door and you walk to the step and stand square to it. The place loads fast: as you come within 16 m of the door, `main.js` starts it coming in (`portal.js` `prefetch()`: the bundle, `dist/portal.js`, 1 MB gzipped; then a light copy of the scan, `assets/splats/sala-thai-lite.sog`, 2 MB; then the full one, `sala-thai.sog`, 14 MB, 1.2 million splats), so at the step there's little or nothing to wait for; what's left shows as a line saying how far along it is. Then you walk into the opening, which fills the view, and the place fades in over it (its own canvas, `.portal-view`, over the planet's and under the UI), facing the same way: the light copy first, the full one replacing it as soon as it's in. The planet stops drawing while you're there. Drag to look, exactly as on the planet (drag right and you turn left, drag down and you look up; the smoke test checks both), tap the ground to walk (within 7.5 m of where you came in, never into the pavilion: `roam`, `keepOut`), WASD and the arrows as extras. The credit is top right, under the bar, with links to the scan and the licence; the hint sits above the bottom buttons. **The door back** stands just behind where you come in, its opening toward the pavilion: turn round and there's the planet at night through it (`assets/splats/sala-back.webp`, the planet from its door's step looking back down the trail, `tools/portal-view.mjs sala --back`; the same window shader as the planet's door, `doorwindow.js`). Tap it and you walk into it and come out on the planet facing down the trail, as you saw through it. Back, Esc or "Step back through" fade the place out over the planet instead, back in the doorway, and you step back out to where you stood, the door in front of you. The place is kept, hidden and not drawn, so stepping in again is instant. It's never freed: Spark's sorting and reads run on after the last frame, freeing what they use throws on the page, and the browser collecting the context stalls the planet for seconds. (Under software WebGL, as in the smoke test, even the idle context slows the planet, so the test steps through the door last.)
- **The splat** comes from the scan on SuperSplat: `tools/splat-place.mjs sala` downloads its levels of detail and keeps the finest within 6 m of the pavilion, coarser out to 14 m and 26 m (`scan.rings`), and nothing nearly transparent, then writes the `.sog` with `splat-transform`, and the light copy from the coarsest level within 16 m, thinned to 45% (`scan.lite`). The scan's own coordinates have y pointing down (as captures do); `portal.js` turns it upright and puts where you come in (`at`) at the origin.
- **The panoramas**: `tools/portal-view.mjs sala` renders six square views of the place with the real thing in headless Chromium (`snapshot()`) and stitches them (run it again whenever the splat or `at` changes; software WebGL takes about 15 minutes); `--back` does the same for the planet from its door's step, from the built site (run it again when the door or the planet around it changes).
- **A new place** (your own Polycam scan, say): add it to `REAL_PLACES` (`splat`, `view`, `credit`, `sky`, and `at`, `face`, `roam`, `keepOut` in the scan's coordinates; find them with a top view of the splat), put the `.ply`/`.spz`/`.sog` in `assets/splats/` with a light copy (keep them under about 15 MB and 2 MB), run `portal-view.mjs` and `portal-view.mjs --back`, and give it a door in `places.js`.
