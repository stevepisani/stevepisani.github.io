# The planet: how it's built

The homepage world, module by module, and how each thing a visitor can do works. Read the part
for whatever you're changing before you change it; the design rules and art direction it all
follows are in `CLAUDE.md` (keep them). How to see your change working: `.claude/skills/verify-world`.

three.js ES modules in `assets/js/world/`, bundled into one file by esbuild (`tools/build-js.mjs`);
`main.js` is the entry. Rapier is the physics engine for the loose things (`physics.js`).

## Modules

- `main.js`: panels, menu, top bar, history (works before and without 3D); then the renderer, picking (hover label + ground ring, click-to-walk, click-to-use; the hover and destination rings are draped on the ground vertex by vertex, over the flagstones and onto the bar's deck (`bar.solids`), and set where you'll really stand: `player.clearOf()` pushes the spot out of every collider by the ring's radius, and you walk there), sitting and leaving, hints, bloom + grade post-processing (desktop only). `#world[data-state]` = loading | walk | seat | camp | hammock | shore | note | fallback.
- `planet.js`: radius-20 planet. `heightAt(dir)` is analytic, so terrain and the player's feet always agree. The visible terrain is a fine cube-sphere (about 0.3 m between vertices on desktop, 0.5 m on phones) with analytic normals and triplanar grain and bump from `textures.js` `groundDetail()`, worn to soil beside the trails; picking uses a coarse invisible copy named `ground` (clicks only need a direction). `place(obj, dir, {heading})` stands anything upright on the sphere; `dirFrom(polar, longitude)` addresses spots (the bar is at the north pole, facing longitude π/2). Also the atmosphere shell, instanced wind-blown grass tufts and pebbles, kept off trails and landmarks (`keepClear` from `places.js`). The lagoon (`POND`): a bowl carved into `heightAt` itself (an irregular outline, `pondReach`; waterline at `SHORE_K`, `shoreAt(phi)`), damp sand at the waterline and silt below it in the terrain colours, and `buildPond()`: a water disc on the sphere at `POND.level` whose edge the bowl's rim hides, dark and glassy (shader ripples bend its normal, the nebula tints it at grazing angles, lamp glints via `lampLit(water, { specular })`), reeds and cattails on the far shore and a few lily pads.
- `player.js`: first-person controller on a sphere. Orientation is a quaternion that is parallel-transported to the new up vector every step; never `lookAt` with a fixed world up (it flips at the poles). Collisions are circles in the tangent plane, tested only against colliders that are actually nearby in 3D (flattened onto the tangent plane, things on the far side of the planet land under your feet). `walkTo(point)` auto-walks and turns you toward your path; timing uses simulation time, not wall clock. Input: one pointer vocabulary for mouse and touch (press without moving = tap, press and move = look), plus WASD/arrows and E/Enter.
- `bar.js`: Steve's, a classic A-frame tiki bar on a lava-rock plinth at the pole: carved tiki posts, steep thatched roof with an upswept ridge and Googie starburst, a neon "Steve's" script in the gable over a lit "World famous" box (dark letters on a glowing face, brass rim, marquee bulbs; `textures.js` `signBox()`), bamboo bar front, tapa-cloth back bar with a chalkboard of favorite drinks hung where you see it from your stool (click it for the recipes), floats and pufferfish lamps, volcano bowl, torches, the robot bartender, palms. `groundY(x, z)` gives the real ground height off the deck (the planet curves away). Seat pose and drink serving live here too: `serve(id)` has the robot shake at once, then at 0.9s slides a mug across the counter to your seat (placed directly under reduced motion); `greet()`/`farewell()` are called on sitting and leaving. `make(recipe, { onStep, onDone })` has the robot make a real drink from `drinks.yml` in front of you, one step at a time: it rolls up to the counter, sets out the glass with its ice (`props.js` `barGlass()`), pours each ingredient from its own tinted bottle (into a tin in the middle of the counter if the drink is shaken, straight into the glass if not; the level rises by each measure), then shakes and strains, stirs or swizzles (the glass frosts), garnishes (`garnishFor()`), and slides it over. Each thing stands under the hand that works it, and a pour stream only shows while the spout is really over the vessel. The bottles it pours Planteray, Campari, Chartreuse and Meletti from are the real ones: `bottle-*` hero models (`"lazy": true` in the manifest, so they load after the reveal: `loadHeroes(…, { lazy: true })`, shaders compiled off to the side, then `bar.setBottles()`), standing in a row at the robot's end of the counter until it picks one up (`REAL` maps a build line to its bottle); everything else pours from a tinted stand-in bottle. Every step has an enter, a per-frame part and a finish, so `skip()` (and a long frame) lands on the finished drink; `cancel()` clears it. `update(t, dt)`: `t` is ambient time (frozen by `?test`), `dt` real frame time for things the guest causes, so they still play under `?test`.
- `places.js`: the trail network (`TRAILS`: a flagstone walk from where you land to the bar, gravel trails from its fork to the rocket, telescope, boat and dish, and on round the back to the campfire, with lanterns along each, walk included, starting a few steps in so none looms as you land; none is a real light: each throws its pool through `lamps.js`), your ship, a Saturn V-style lander (`rocket.js`), on a lava-rock landing pad with chasing edge lights and floodlights up the hull, a brass refractor telescope on a wooden tripod, the campfire (`camp.js`), the radio dish ("Get in touch": Contact), the canoe (pulled up out of the lagoon, bow in the shallows; the lagoon is a collider, so you stop at the waterline), round the lagoon (`LAGOON` azimuths; a spur trail comes down beside the canoe) a striped hammock between two palms (leaning away from each other) with a book left open in it, and two more palms at the water's edge in front of it, leaning out over the lagoon and apart, which frame the view when you lie back (you climb in, lie back and read: the Reading list; see below) and messages in bottles washed up at the waterline (crouch behind them and write one, then throw it into space: see below), a little pile of flat stones where the lagoon trail comes down to the water (skip one: see below), a tiki signpost in the corner of the fork whose arrow boards point along the real trails (wayfinding, not clickable), and seeded scatter (kept off the trails: lava rock, palms, and `decor.js` `shrub()`s, one merged mesh per shrub) (`SPOTS` holds their positions). Interactables are `{ id, label, verb, object, extra, point, approach, radius }`: click `object` (or anything in `extra`), walk to `approach`, then use it; an `id` that's a panel id (`contact`, `shelf`, `launch`) opens that panel, so a landmark is a shortcut to a section that's also in the top bar and menu. The bartender mentions them (`_data/bar.yml`).
- `rocket.js`: your ship, `saturnLander()`: a Saturn V cut down to about 3.7 m and built like the Outer Wilds ship. It keeps what makes it recognisable (the stack of stages narrowing to the command module and a red escape tower, the black-and-white roll pattern, "USA" and a flag, five engine bells, four fins) on a squat lathe body whose UVs run by height, so one canvas paints the livery in metres, plus hand-built wear (panel seams, rivet rows, patch plates, scuffs) and the SJPJr badge from the favicon, drawn in once it loads. Outer Wilds touches: four sprung landing legs (hinge, piston strut, round foot), a glowing porthole cockpit in a riveted brass ring, a hatch with a lit window and a ladder to the ground, thruster quads, a dish antenna. The front is local -z; `faceFront(x, z)` turns it to the path.
- `camp.js`: the campfire, Outer Wilds style. `buildCampfire()`: lava-rock ring, glowing coals, a log teepee (turned so no log stands end-on in front of you), two flames, sparks, log benches, and company: Rodin's Thinker (a nod to Philadelphia's Rodin Museum; a `thinker` hero model, `props.js` `thinkerFallback()` as the stand-in) seated on his rock in one place round the fire and the moai behind the log in another, either side of yours (`places.js` sinks both into the ground, which curves away from the camp's plane), a bag of marshmallows; `seatToward(x, z)` puts your bench toward the trail and returns the seat poses. `createRoaster(camera)`: the stick you hold (on the camera, so it follows your look); press and hold (Space on keyboards) reaches it into the flame, it toasts by distance to the hot spot (cream, golden, brown, black), catches fire past dark brown, and `eat()` gives a `verdict()`; each marshmallow comes with a line from `likes` in `_data/bar.yml`. In `main.js`, `sitAtFire()`/`leaveFire()` use the same continuous `flyPath` body motion as the bar; "Eat it" (or "Blow it out") bottom-right, "Leave the fire" bottom-left, Esc leaves; the roast hint retires once something's toasted.
- `paths.js`: trails built vertex by vertex on the analytic surface, so nothing floats or sinks: `sampleTrail(points)` (a smooth, gently meandering curve), a crushed-shell gravel ribbon with ragged edges (`trailTexture()`), flagstones draped on the terrain (top a few cm proud, sides running into the ground; `layout()` lays every slab by its real outline and never lets two touch: a slab that won't fit is tried smaller, then left out, so there's always a gravel joint; edge pebbles likewise), pebble borders, lantern spots, and `trailEdgeFn()` (distance to the nearest trail edge, for grass, scatter and ground wear). Triangles must face outward: a ribbon or slab wound the other way is culled and vanishes.
- `decor.js`: the layered density real tiki bars have (from Smuggler's Cove, Three Dots and a Dash, Trader Vic's, Tiki-Ti): a backlit rum wall (instanced lathe bottles with labels and caps) on every shelf, crossed paddles and a carved mask on the tapa, a sagging net of glass floats under the roof, a grass fringe and festoon bulbs on the front beam, rope trim on the bar top, the robot's station (citrus, tins, jigger, bitters, swizzles and umbrellas, towel), and monstera, red ti and fern beds around the plinth (entrance left open). Repeats are instanced and it adds no lights, so phones pay little. Leaves, net and rope textures live in `textures.js`.
- `robot.js`: the bartender, a 1950s tin-toy robot built from primitives, printed like the real ones: `textures.js` `tinLitho()` lithographs every panel (teal enamel, a cream and coral border line, printed rivets and seams, vents; chevrons round the skirt). `PALETTE.tinTeal` is bluer than teal on purpose, so it still reads teal under the amber lamps. Box head with a glowing Grog-o-Meter dial under a red visor, glass dome, an antenna on a coil spring, aloha shirt with a paper umbrella in the pocket, a lei of plumeria flowers (`plumeria()`) draped the way a real one lies: flat on the shoulder plate outside the head (which overhangs the neck), then down the chest in a U; a bow tie on the collar plate, three blinking belly lamps, jointed arms with pincers, a shaker and a bar towel, and one wheel on a chrome bellows under a red tin mudguard. Poses (rest, greet, shake, pour) are eased per joint; to make a drink, `hold()` puts a bottle, bar spoon, swizzle stick or garnish in a pincer (hung tipped down from it, so reaching out over a glass tips a bottle's neck into it) and `reach()` solves the arm joints (coordinate descent on the real joints, for where the robot is headed) to put the held thing's tip at a point, with `stir` / `swizzle` wiggles; `beckon(guest)` turns and waves as you walk up; left alone at rest it wipes down the counter every nine seconds or so; `greet(spot, guest)` rolls over and turns to face you when you sit; with reduced motion it snaps between poses and nothing moves on its own. A `robot` hero model can replace the body only.
- `sky.js`: dim fbm nebula dome, twinkling stars, a cool distant star (the moonlight's source), shooting stars, and two bodies lit by that star in their own shaders: a ringed gas giant (warped turbulent bands, a storm, soft terminator, limb darkening, rings and globe shadowing each other) low on the left as you land, and a cratered moon (`textures.js` `moonMaps()`, Lommel-Seeliger shading, earthshine) up on the right, in tonight's real phase: it has its own light direction, set from `site.js` `moonTonight()` (the lunar age from a known new moon, no API): from behind it at new, over your shoulder at full, from the right while waxing and the left while waning; it stays where it is in the sky. The gas giant's phase is physics: place it by angle to `sunDir`. Distant bodies set `fog: false`.
- `materials.js`: the palette (`PALETTE`) and every surface: procedural canvas textures (wood grain, bamboo, thatch, lava rock, tapa cloth, rattan, scuffed tin) on PBR materials, `enamel()` and `chrome()` for the robot, `glow()` for things that should bloom, `restyle()` for loaded Kenney models. Use these; don't introduce ad-hoc colours.
- `props.js`: procedural tiki props: carved tiki (a height-map face displaced into real geometry), glass floats in rope nets, fallen coconuts (`coconut()`), pufferfish lamps, tiki mugs, volcano bowl, bamboo tiki torches (tapered pole with nodes, rope lashing, a flared split-bamboo cup round a fuel canister and wick), palms with ringed trunks, alpha-textured fronds and a couple of dead ones hanging, lava rocks, moai; out on the planet, `radioDish()` (a retro radio telescope: pedestal, yoke, parabolic dish with seams, ribbed back, feed horn on struts, equipment hut) and `outriggerCanoe()` (hull with raised ends, float on two booms, paddle), `barGlass(kind)` (rocks, collins or coupe, with fill, ice, foam, frost and garnish) and `garnishFor(name)` (pineapple wedge, orange slice, mint sprig, coffee beans) for the drinks the robot makes, `hammock(a, b, up)` (striped canvas, `textures.js` `hammockCloth()`, fan lines, an open book with printed pages, `bookPage()`; `userData.hammock` lets you get in: the cloth, lines and book hang in a `swing` group that swings about the tie line as a damped pendulum (`kick()`, `update(dt)`), `setLoad(k)` sags it under your weight, `point(u, lift, across)` gives spots on it in the swing's frame), `bookStack()`, `messageBottles()`, `signpost(boards)` (lettering from `signLettering()`). `fire.js`: shader flames on crossed quads + flickering lights (`light: 0` = flame only).
- `lamps.js`: lamp light without lights. Lanterns and the walk's torches `addLamp()` a position and strength; `lampLitTree()` gives every standard material (`lampLit(m, { specular })` adds their glints, for water) on the planet and around the landmarks a shader loop that adds their warm pools (soft falloff, wrapped Lambert, a little flicker from `updateLamps(t)`, max 48, 5.5 m range). Same cost however many lamps, so phones get lit paths too; use it rather than adding real lights out on the planet.
- `sound.js`: sound, all synthesised with Web Audio (no audio files), each source placed on the planet with three.js `PositionalAudio` so it gets louder as you walk up: a quiet generated exotica loop at the bar (rolled vibraphone chords with motor tremolo, upright bass, bongos, the odd whistled bird call), the fire's rumble and crackles, water lapping at the lagoon, crickets everywhere; and effects from `bar.setSfx()` (the shaker, ice into a glass, pours while the stream is showing) the hammock's ropes creaking, and thuds and splashes wherever a loose thing lands (one movable source). The speaker button in the top bar (`#sound-btn`, next to the sections or the Menu button) starts it; it's off until asked for, remembered in localStorage `world-sound` (a remembered "on" starts on the first click or key, as browsers require), and suspended while the tab is hidden.
- `physics.js`: Rapier (WebAssembly) for the loose things: the fallen coconuts under the palms (`props.js` `coconut()`, `bar.loose` under the front palms and `places.loose` under the scattered ones) and two glass floats washed up by the bottles. Rapier has one gravity vector, so world gravity is zero and each body is pulled toward the centre every step (9.8 m/s², like the stones). Colliders: the ground as a trimesh of the analytic surface (an icosphere, about 0.5 m between vertices, lagoon bowl included), the player's collider circles stood up as posts (`height` where a thrown thing should clear them, e.g. the counter; `walkOnly` ones, like the lagoon's waterline and the staff-only area, are skipped), the bar's plinth (`bar.solids`), and you, a kinematic capsule at your feet, so walking into a coconut knocks it along. In the lagoon (`pondK < 1`, under `RADIUS + POND.level`): buoyancy by how deep it's in (`buoyancy`: coconuts 1.6, floats 4), water drag, and a slow current back to the sand. On dry ground, slow things drag to a stop in the grass and sleep (Rapier won't on a slope); they start asleep, lying where they were put, until disturbed. `on('thud' | 'splash', at, k)` for sounds (`sound.js`) and ripples (`stones.js` `ripple`). Long frames run up to four sub-steps. It loads after the reveal (`load()`, `import()` of the engine) and nothing can be picked up until then. In `main.js` (the carry block): click one and you walk over and pick it up, held at arm's length (`CARRY`, on the camera) while you walk about; "Hold to throw" bottom-right (or Space): held longer is harder (full at 1 s), it goes where you look; "Put it down" bottom-left or Esc sets it at your feet, and anything that takes you out of the walk state puts it down first. The throw hint retires after the first throw. You and the skipping stones stay hand-written (`player.js`, `stones.js`).
- `booksky.js`: the book sky. Every book Steve has read (`/library.json`: `read`, from `tools/library.mjs`; no telling how he read it) is a star over the hammock, grouped by shelf, the shelves of /bookshelf and `_data/books.yml` in the same order. Each shelf is a disc just big enough for its books, and the discs sit in two rows in shelf order, left to right, like a bookcase (the split that keeps the rows most even), filling the window between the shore palms (`WIN`: 30° each side, 16° up and down, its middle 5° below the lying view's centre; the fronds hang across the top). With more books every star sits a little closer (`GAP` from 3.4° down to no less than 1.0°) rather than spreading behind the palms. Each shelf's name is in letter-spaced caps in its colour, above its patch on the top row and below it on the bottom one. A star's colour is its shelf's (`hue` in `books.yml`, pale enough to read as starlight), its size the book's length (8 to 13 px; 10 with no length); each is a small core that just blooms, with faint rays. A series of two or more inside a shelf is a constellation: its books in order, joined by faint lines, its name beside it once you've leaned in on that shelf (a name is left out if it would cover another's, biggest series first). What he's reading now (`now`) are comets crossing the whole field, three in flight at a time (`COMETS`), most recently played first: each takes a minute and a half (`CROSS`), fading in and out at the ends, then the next takes its turn (under reduced motion the first three sit mid-way, still). Laid out deterministically (hashed by book and series). Stars are at fixed world directions, on the sky's shell (the group rides on the camera like `sky.js`). The cone is centred on the lying view (`main.js`): you lie across the hammock looking up and out over the lagoon, between the two shore palms, whose trunks rise up the left and right edges; on a phone the middle of it is in view and you drag for the sides. If you move the hammock, those palms or the lying pose, re-check that framing (`verify-world`). `pick(x, y, …, reach, inRegion)` finds the star under a tap (screen space), `regionAt(x, y)` the shelf (its name, padded to 44 px, or its patch), `aim(r)` where to look and how narrow a view to lean in on it, `setFocus(r)` fades the other shelves back and brings up its series' names, `select(b)` lights one star and its constellation. Data: `/library.json`, fetched once the planet's up; no data, no stars.
- `stones.js`: skipping stones. `stonePile()` for the shore; `createSkipper(scene)` flies them with no physics engine: a point under the planet's gravity (toward its centre), in 1/240 s steps; meeting the water (a sphere `RADIUS + POND.level` from the centre, inside the waterline `shoreAt`), it skips if it comes in under about 25° and over 2.2 m/s across, keeping 78% of its speed across and 55% of its speed down (as up), and leaves a ripple ring; otherwise it sinks with a plop, or lands on the sand (on the far side: "all the way across"). In `main.js` (`goToShore()` / `leaveShore()`, state `shore`): walk down to the pile and crouch at the water, a stone in your hand; press and hold (canvas, Space, or the "Hold to throw" button bottom-right) to wind up, let go to throw: held longer is harder (full at 1.1 s), where you look is where it goes, released low and level just over the water's edge the way stones skip. A short hold sinks, the sweet spot (about 0.7 s) gets 4 or 5 skips, full power makes the far shore. How it went is said in the bubble, your best is kept in localStorage `world-best-skips`, and the hint retires after the first skip. "Leave the shore" bottom-left or Esc stands you back up where you walked in.
- `note.js`: a message in a bottle, all in your hands (camera space, so the camera never moves for it). `createNoteRitual()`: `open()` picks up a bottle (`props.js` `messageBottle()`), pulls the cork, tips it and slides the letter out, sets the bottle down and unrolls the letter (a segmented plane wound round a tightening roll, `set(u)` from rolled to flat) where it fits the view; `rect()` is where it is on screen; `close(text, signed)` paints the words onto the paper (parchment canvas, ruled lines, Caveat), rolls it up, puts it back in and corks it; `throwIt()` sends it in an arc out over the lagoon and then, at the top of the arc, the planet lets go and it drifts off into space, tumbling. The letter glows a little (it's out of reach of the lanterns). Each stage is a timed step (reduced motion lands on the end of each). In `main.js` (`goToBottles()` / `leaveBottles()`, state `note`): crouch behind the bottles facing across the water and up a little; it opens at once, and the `#note` form sits exactly over the unrolled paper (`placeNote()`, every frame, `--gap` = the paper's line spacing) so you write on it: a transparent textarea in Caveat, "Signed (optional)", "N left", "Put it back" and "Roll it up" (Ctrl/Cmd+Enter). Rolling it up sends it to `public.bottles` (unapproved until Steve approves it; the 200-waiting cap's message is said if it's full); then "Throw it" bottom-right (Space/Enter/E) and the bubble says where it went; "Write another" starts again. Esc while writing puts the letter back; Esc otherwise or "Leave the shore" bottom-left stands you up. Tests (`?test`, automated browsers) never write to the table (`window.__lastBottle`).
- `fireflies.js`: everyone else on the planet right now, as fireflies: Supabase Realtime presence on the `planet` channel over a plain WebSocket (the Phoenix protocol, no client library). You join under a random key made fresh per page load and track only a rounded direction on the planet, when you've moved about a metre (at most every 4 s); others drift over to theirs a metre and a bit above the ground, bobbing and blinking slowly (still under reduced motion). At most 200, one `Points` object. It leaves while the tab is hidden and reconnects with backoff.
- `render.js`: pmndrs `postprocessing` + `n8ao`: AO → HDR-only mipmap bloom (threshold 1) → AgX tone mapping → vignette + grain → SMAA, then an untouched `overlay` scene on top (the menu card in your hands). Phones skip AO, grain, and shadows.
- `hero.js`: optional hero props (AI-generated or hand-made GLBs) in `assets/models/hero/`, listed in `manifest.json`; each slot falls back to its procedural version. Slots, prompts, manifest options (`height`, `rotateY`, `roughness`, `tint`, `brightness`, `glow`) and specs: `tools/models/HERO_PROMPTS.md`; sources and licences: `tools/models/README.md`.

## What a visitor does, and how it works

- Content stays HTML: the top bar and menu items are `<a href>`s (JS intercepts them to open `<template id="panel-<id>">`, and serves a drink if you're seated), so it works without WebGL and is crawlable. Without WebGL2 the menu is the page. Once the panels are in charge, `main.js` points those links at `#<id>` (their real hrefs, some outbound: the résumé on Google Drive, mailto:) and `fallback()` restores them, so nothing in the menu or the drink animation ever leaves the page; the no-WebGL menu marks its outbound items ↗. Outbound links inside panels say where they go ("on Google Drive ↗").
- "Make me one" (`startMaking()` in `main.js`): the robot makes that recipe (`bar.make`), and a caption above the bottom buttons (`#making`: the drink, step n of N, what it's doing, progress dots; shown on phones too, where the bubble isn't) narrates it. "Skip to the drink" bottom-right or Esc jumps to the finished drink; "Leave the bar" stops it. From anywhere on the planet it walks you to your stool first. While it's made you lean in over the counter (the view narrows to about half its field, two thirds on phones so the whole workspace fits) and your eyes follow the work: the vessel being poured into, the shaker while it shakes, the glass for the rest, then the drink as it slides over (`focus()` on the job, eased like a head turn; snapped under reduced motion); you sit back once it's in front of you. Dragging to look is off while you lean in, and the menu (card, Menu button, E) finishes the drink first. Ordering from the menu meanwhile finishes the drink first; the bartender's chatter waits.
- The book sky (`zoomTo()` / `zoomOut()`, `openStar()` / `closeStar()` in `main.js`): only from the hammock, lying back. On the whole sky, hovering a shelf says its name and how many books; tapping it (its name or its patch) leans in: the view turns to it and narrows to fit it (`camera.fov` eases from `BASE_FOV` to its `aim()` fov, 18° at the narrowest, over 1.1 s; snapped under reduced motion), the swing stills under you (the lying pose blends to the hammock at rest, or the stars would swing too), the other shelves fade, and its books' titles come up beside their stars (`#sky-labels`, DOM labels laid out again only when the view moves: as many as fit without touching each other or the buttons, the open book's first, then newest first). A drag pans, slowed by how narrow the view is. `#sky-shelf` above the bottom buttons names the shelf, steps ‹ › from shelf to shelf in order, and × leans back out. Leaned in, tapping a star opens `#star-card` in the strip's place (lower left on wide screens): cover (or its title on a square in the shelf's colour), title, author, series, "Science Fiction · Read March 2026" (just the shelf when undated) or "Reading now, 42% through", "Hear a sample" where there is one (Audible's public clip, `site.js` `playSample()`, one at a time), and ‹ › through the rest of that shelf, newest first, the order of /bookshelf (turning to a star that's off screen). A comet is a book from the whole sky too. Esc or × puts the card away first, the next Esc leans out; leaning in adds a `#sky` history entry, so Back leans out too (and nothing links into it); getting up leans out. The Reading list's shelf headings (`data-shelf`) lean in on that shelf from the hammock, and go to /bookshelf#shelf anywhere else. Hints: "Every star above you is a book Steve has read, grouped by kind…" when the reading list first closes (retires on the first lean in), then "Tap a star to see the book." (retires on the first card).
- The hammock (`lieInHammock()` / `getOutOfHammock()` in `main.js`, state `hammock`): the same kind of continuous first-person body motion as the bar and the fire. Step up to its side, turn round and sit on its edge (it takes your weight: `setLoad`), swing your legs up and in and lie back across it, looking up and out over the water between the two shore palms, at the book sky; it swings from you getting in and you swing with it until it settles (the lying pose lives in the swing's frame; not under reduced motion). You pick up the book left in it, held open above you, and the Reading list (`panel-shelf`) pours out of it. Closing the list lowers the book to your chest; "Reading list" bottom-right (or E) lifts it again, "Get up" bottom-left or Esc gets you out: the reverse, with the book back where it was, ending where you walked in, facing the hammock. Drag to look around while you lie there. The half turns (standing to sitting on the edge, and back) take 1.8 s, and as you swing your legs in you turn to face along it, then settle back across it, so the view sweeps round rather than tipping over the top of your head (which would flip it); getting in and out both peak at about 133°/s, inside the 140°/s limit.
- Asking the bartender (`#chat` / `#chat-log` in `index.html`, the chat block in `main.js`): while you're seated with nothing else open, a question box sits at the bottom (the Leave button's row on desktop, just above it on phones) and the conversation stacks up right above it as a short transcript, your questions on the right and the robot's answers on the left, on phones too; the oldest fade out at the top and scroll, and neither covers the menu card. An answer shows as it's said (the function streams it as plain text), with a pulsing ellipsis until the first words land; the transcript goes when the box does and comes back with it. The first question stops the rotating chatter. It posts the conversation (last 12 turns, `stream: true`) to the Supabase function `supabase/functions/bartender` (Claude Haiku 4.5, `max_tokens` 160, asked to stay under 40 words), which knows the site from `/bartender.json` (`bartender.json` at the root, built from `_data`: profile, career, menu, drinks, posts, lab, books, the bartender's lines), answers in a sentence or two, and keeps to the database's limits (`bartender_take()`: 1,000 replies a month, about $5, and 20 questions an hour per visitor, a daily-salted hash of the IP; `bartender_spent()` logs tokens in `bartender_usage`). If it can't answer, the robot's line says the bar's closed for a moment. Typing in the box doesn't move you (arrow keys, WASD), and Esc there only leaves the box.
- Ordering at the bar (`order()` in `main.js`): choosing an item puts the menu back down so you can watch the robot shake the drink and slide it over (`bar.serve(id, onServed)`), then the mug lifts to you, tipped so you look into the drink, and the section's panel pours open out of the mug's mouth (`.panel.is-pouring`, a clip-path circle from `--ox/--oy`). Closing it sets the mug back down and the menu comes back up. Drinks come in the tiki mug (open top, so there's a drink to look into), coloured per section; the parrot and pineapple mugs dress the counter and shelf. Reduced motion skips the flights.
- At the bar the menu is a real card on the counter (`bar.menu`), and it is the HTML menu: `textures.js` `paintMenuCard()` paints its face from the laid-out `.menu__card` (every box, line of text, colour, focus state and its proportions), repainted on resize, pick-up and put-down. Sitting down leaves it there; clicking it (or E/Enter, or the Menu button) picks it up: `main.js` flies the 3D card to the exact rectangle the HTML menu will fill, facing the camera, then fades the HTML menu in over it and hides the card only once the page is opaque (no frame with neither). While it flies it's drawn in `render.js`'s `overlay` pass, after the grade, going from how the lamps tint it on the counter (`bar.menu.light()`) to the page's own colours, so the handoff doesn't change a pixel. × / Esc fly it back down. Clicking the card from anywhere walks you over, sits you down and picks it up. The camera never moves for this; reduced motion skips the flight.

## Models, libraries, tests, performance

- Models: Kenney CC0 props merged into `assets/models/props.glb` by `tools/build-models.mjs`, which only includes models the world code clones by a literal `prop('<kit>_<name>')` (just the coconut now; the rest stay in `tools/models/` as sources). To add one, drop its GLB in `tools/models/<kit>/`, clone it with `prop('<kit>_<name>', scale)`, then run `npm install && npm run models`.
- Three.js, postprocessing, n8ao and Rapier are pinned once, in `package.json`; import three's addons as `three/addons/...` (the package maps them) and esbuild bundles and minifies them with the rest. A new world module needs nothing but its import.
- Testing hooks (`tools/smoke.mjs` drives them; see `.claude/skills/verify-world`): `window.__sceneReady`, `window.__world` (state, menuHeld, cardFlying, cameraFlying, player, pipeline, SPOTS, bar, interactables, sitDown, leaveBar, pickUpMenu, putDownMenu, pick, sitAtFire, leaveFire, eatIt, roaster, roast, lieInHammock, getOutOfHammock, lying, goToShore, leaveShore, skipper, stoneInHand, throwStone(holdSeconds) (and `window.__lastThrow`, how the last one went), make(i), making, note (go, leave, step, write(text, signed), throwIt, putBack, ritual: its `stage`), fireflies (count, connected, `fake(key, dir)` to place one), sky (the book sky: `books`, `regions`, `count`), stars (how many), zoom(slug), zoomOut, zoomed (the shelf leaned in on), zoomK (0 to 1), stepShelf(±1), stepBook(±1), openStar(i), closeStar, starCard (the open card's title), physics (once loaded: `items`, each with its `body`, `wet`, `held`, `home`), carrying (the label of what's in your hand), grab(i), goGrab(i) (walk over and pick up `physics.items[i]`), toss(holdSeconds) (and `window.__lastToss`), putDown, job: the drink in progress, whose `seek(k)` holds it on step k and `steps` lists them; `bar.robot.debug()` gives each hand's reach target and tip), `?test=1` freezes ambient time, disables motion, and allows large time steps so walks finish under software rendering. Under SwiftShader, emulate `prefers-reduced-motion` too: CSS fades barely advance while WebGL frames take seconds.
- Performance: the carved tiki face is drawn sharp and blurred once (a canvas `filter` on every shape cost seconds of load); point-light shadows (the campfire) only update within 28 m of the camera; hero textures are 256–512px except the bar's posts (1024 colour). Measure before adding weight.
